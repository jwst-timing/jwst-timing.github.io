Calibration

Timing science needs two things: precise spacing between frames, and knowing when a time series starts, to well under a second. Our first goal was to test both.

Calibrating the clock of JWST

We observed the deeply eclipsing double white dwarf ZTF J153932.16+502738.8, whose 6.9-minute orbit and sharp eclipses make it a natural clock. Its eclipse times are tracked from the ground with GPS-synchronised instruments, so JWST's timestamps can be compared directly with them.

Over two epochs, JWST's absolute timing accuracy averages 0.12 ± 0.06 s. That means JWST can be used for sub-second timing down to about the 100 ms level.

Shaw et al. 2025, AJ 169, 21: journal, arXiv:2411.02238
