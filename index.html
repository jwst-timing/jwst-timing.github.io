Infrared astronomy at sub-second speed

JWST is the largest and most sensitive infrared space telescope yet built. Read out in small detector subarrays, it can sample a source every ~0.1 s. We work to make that timing trustworthy, and use it to study how compact objects, jets and small bodies vary on the fastest timescales.

What we do

The rapid time-domain infrared remains largely unexplored. Our consortium enables and exploits timing studies with JWST, from calibrating the observatory's clock to measuring lags between the infrared and other wavebands. Current interests include accretion onto black holes and neutron stars, relativistic jets, stellar occultations by Solar System bodies, pulsating sources and more.
